# Learning to Weave collection

Source: Deborah Chandler, *Learning to Weave*, revised edition, Interweave Press, 1995. Transcribed from the supplied scan. Page references below are the book’s printed page numbers (PDF page = printed page + 4).

## Scope and conventions

- The collection contains 108 entries: complete draft examples, specified color sequences, treadling variations, and editable expansions of the book’s block diagrams. Repeated illustrations of the same draft are combined and cross-referenced.
- Photographs without a complete threading/treadling, yarn experiments, finishing instructions, and open-ended homework designs do not specify reproducible drafts and are not reconstructed.
- Source shaft numbers are one-based. The library converts them to its internal indexing and consolidates identical sheds into treadles. This may change treadle positions from the page, but preserves the lifted shafts and their order.
- Overshot and summer & winter entries marked with `tabby` explicitly alternate pattern and tabby picks. Printed diagrams often omit these tabby picks. Profile expansions contain their tabby picks already.
- Display colors and yarn thicknesses are illustrative. Lettered color sequences preserve their order. Waffle sequence A is monochrome; B–G preserve the book’s two-, three-, and four-color arrangements.
- Profile expansions use one weave unit per diagram square. They are labeled in the interface rather than presented as full drafts printed in the book. The lace design sketches on p. 174 are expanded with sample proportions based on the drawn spots. Other unlabeled patterns have descriptive catalog titles.
- Threading studies complete a threading-only illustration with the adjacent teaching tie-up and a stated treadling. Technique samples state the choices needed to illustrate a method.
- Balancing edge threads and partial repeats are retained. Editor previews start with one copy because an excerpt is not necessarily a seamless repeat.
- Flat drawdowns show interlacements, not wet finishing, deflected honeycomb outlines, lace openings, double-weave layers, folds, or shuttle paths. The double-weave descriptions specify shuttle use.
- The ten-shaft extended twill on p. 152 is retained as a design study, with the book’s practical limitation noted.
- Periwinkle is attributed by Chandler to Marguerite Davison’s *A Handweaver’s Pattern Book*, p. 131; this secondary attribution appears with the entry.

The source PDF, page images, and extracted book text are not shipped with the website.

## Maintenance and verification

The data is in `learning-to-weave.js`; integration and lazy draft construction are in `pattern-library.html`. No PDF-reading or external service is needed at runtime. Book drafts are built only when selected.

Run `node --test tests/pattern-library.test.cjs` from the repository root. Checks cover unique routes, complete thread assignments, valid sheds, source lift-plan preservation, tabby interlacing, selected source sequences, double-weave lifting, and transfer payloads. Browser checks should exercise all entries, the three render styles, direct pattern links, narrow screens, and Open in Drafter.

The original grids were visually reviewed. Longer solid drawdowns were also cross-checked cell-by-cell against their threading/lift plans; diagram separator rows were excluded from the cropped-diamond examples on p. 138. The six-shaft twill/plain-weave example on p. 151 contains a stray dark mark below a printed 6; the numbered shaft and consistent twill sequence determine that end.
