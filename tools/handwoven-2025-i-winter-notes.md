# Handwoven, Winter 2025

Source: `2025 I Winter.pdf`, 84 PDF pages (printed pages 1-80).

The transcription contains 24 drafts: four Star of Bethlehem color-and-weave studies, two Rebecca Winter Daily Drafts, Magic Ruffles Scarf, five Garden Path Towels, Misty Mountains Runner, three Secret Passage variants, three Blueberry Waffle colorways, Making Waves Runner, two Dicey Dish Towels colorways, and both Birds of a Feather versions.

Numeric shaft glyphs, treadle marks, and colored squares were read from PDF geometry after visual inspection of rendered pages. The two Daily Drafts are embedded raster diagrams and need separate raster reading. All yarn colors approximate the printed yarns. Doubled mixed-color wefts in Dicey Dish Towels are shown as averaged colors, and space-dyed/gradient yarns use representative shades.

Full-width threading counts checked against the issue: Magic Ruffles 192; Garden Path 366; Misty Mountains 179; Secret Passage 280; Blueberry Waffle 504; Making Waves 130; Dicey Dish Towels 408 doubled ends; Birds of a Feather 264 and 265. Floating selvedges are not added to the heddled draft. All numbered threading and pick brackets are expanded; sections specified only in inches use complete design units instead of invented pick counts. Measured hems and body lengths therefore remain instructions, rather than promises of full project-length lift plans. Tabby appears explicitly where required, including every thick overshot pick in Misty Mountains.

Secret Passage's two four-shaft entries deliberately show both printed tie-up notations. Their sheds match exactly; the six-treadle skeleton needs at most two treadles together and the eight-treadle version uses one. The printed eight-shaft pebble-weave version includes its separate tube, layer-exchange, and two-layer sheds. Drafts describe but do not physically simulate the tunnel, finished honeycomb cells, or wool-induced collapse.

Garden Path uses Natural warp in all five drafts, with the actual Heather, Royal, Sage, Plum Green, Royal Lilac, Carolina Blue, and Natural wefts. The colored squares, rather than a treadle-based color guess, distinguish the color changes. Towels 4 and 5 follow the printed reverse-side tie-ups, as the article's notes specify.

The complete October 3 shadow draft is 44 ends by 44 picks. Its seemingly checkered tie-up has essential non-checkered turning-point ties. It reconstructs the source drawdown to within the raster's antialiasing/grid noise.

The complete October 18 draft is 220 ends by 220 picks, including two identical 110-end/pick repeats. It is read directly from the 635-by-635-pixel embedded full diagram, not expanded from the adjacent 52-block profile (which deliberately omits turning-point units). Subpixel glyph sampling identifies all 220 threading and 220 treadling assignments consistently under three slightly shifted crop boxes. Reconstructing the source cloth from these assignments and its printed tie-up matches 99.71% of source cell centers; the remaining cells reflect JPEG/grid antialiasing. The first weft pick is light; the rightmost threading end is dark.

Omitted material:

- *A Shaggy-Bag Story*, pp. 22-24: Ghiordes knots on a plain-weave ground, a hand-manipulation-only project.
- *Dip-Dyed Shetland Fringe*, pp. 68-72: hand-worked passementerie and dyeing, without a numeric shaft draft.
- The Zephira/Zephyr Yarn Lab, pp. 73-75: photographs and descriptions, without printed numeric drafts.
- Historic-draft promotional thumbnails, p. 63: external Handweaving.net references, not additional project drafts.
- The reader-guide illustration, p. 80: a generic explanation of draft reading rather than an additional issue project.
