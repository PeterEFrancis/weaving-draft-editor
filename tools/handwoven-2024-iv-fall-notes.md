# Handwoven Fall 2024 transcription notes

37 editable drafts: 17 numeric examples from **The Draft: What Is a Twill?** (pp. 33-35), plus 20 project drafts and separately printed color/treadling versions.

## Included projects

- Vis-à-Vis Runner (Sheila O'Hara, pp. 36-38): full 16-shaft satin-damask threading and all 1,350 picks. The two doubled, loom-controlled selvedges on shaft 16 are represented by their four physical threads: 684 physical threads, corresponding to the source's 682 working ends. All 2x, 3x, 5x, 13x, and 14x lift-plan brackets and the eight Safari and three Pistachio weft picks are explicit.
- Autumn Jewel Scarf (Véronique Perrot, pp. 40-42): complete 344-end diversified-plain-weave threading and 2,168 picks, including all 15 complete body repetitions. Fine and thick yarns retain separate thicknesses. The unspecified random dark-blue/purple fine-warp mixture and variegated wool are represented by their printed key colors.
- Rainbow Sherbet Towels (Malynda Allen, pp. 44-46): four color versions, each with 462 ends and 644 picks. The measured warp totals are 119 Lupine Blue, 114 Lavender, 112 Salmon, and 117 Mint. The source calls for random threading order within each held three-end color group; a deterministic permissible ordering is used. Every 5x warp bracket, 3x/6x weft-color bracket, and printed pick count is expanded. The numeric treadling, including the mock-plain-weave hems, is read directly from the draft.
- Dizzy Drones Honeycomb Pillows (Rebecca Fox, pp. 48-50): the common 296-end threading and both distinct 115-pick design sequences. All 4x brackets are expanded. Each 6-inch plain-weave end section is stored as its two-pick repeat and should be repeated to length. Thick denim strips have separate palette/thickness entries from thin white/blue yarn. The drawdown does not reproduce wet-finished honeycomb relief.
- A Taste of Sonoma Scarf (Robin Monogue, pp. 51-53): complete 201-end threading and 1,386 picks, including all 23 body repetitions, colored end borders, tabby, and the three-/five-pick pattern groups. Pattern wool and ground Tencel have different thicknesses.
- Blue Willow Towels (Susie Hodges, pp. 54-56): complete 310-end threading and 347-pick illustrated design expansion. All 3x/9x threading and 2x/8x treadling brackets are expanded. The two-inch hems are stored as their two-pick repeat. Comparison with the official lift-plan supplement confirms every nonempty numeric row; the supplement has unmarked rows at picks 87, 167, 247, and 327, which the magazine draft resolves.
- Corduroy Patch Pillow (Sue Anne Sullivan, pp. 57-59): common 465-end threading, 419-pick front, and the separately printed four-pick back repeat. The complete front lift sequence matches the official numeric supplement. Doubled wool pattern wefts have distinct thicknesses. Pile cutting is described; the drawdown shows the uncut interlacements.
- Taqueté All Day Tote (Annette Swan Schipf, pp. 60-62): full 177-end threading and the publisher's complete **536-pick numeric lift plan**, including source color bars and the separate cotton/rug-wool header and footer yarns. The printed magazine diagram differs at pick 77 and several repeat extents; the complete official plan is used to resolve those discrepancies. Paired skeleton treadles are stored as their exact raised-shaft unions.
- Gilded Amethyst Scarf (Laurie Brown, pp. 64-67): complete 259-end threading and four separately labeled treadlings: advancing point (54 picks), straight draw (16), point draw including its continuation (32), and rosepath (28). Gold pattern picks and Amethyst tie-down picks are explicit. The designer's additional full-length random treadling is an optional external variation, not another printed draft.
- Mod Block Towels (Tracy Kaestner, pp. 68-71): three color versions of the complete 576-end threading and 1,524-pick standard treadling. The five 252-pick pattern blocks and two 132-pick hem/border sections follow each towel's published colors. The standard and skeleton tie-ups produce the same lift plan and are not duplicated. The official numeric supplement uses `0` to denote treadle 10, not a shaft or an empty shed.

The supplied magazine remains the source for threading, repeats, project attribution, yarn keys, and version separation. PDF pages and numeric charts were rendered and visually inspected. Machine extraction reads the PDF's vector marks and glyph positions; it does not infer drafts from fabric photographs.

## Deliberate omissions

- Show Your Team Spirit (pp. 22-24) uses finnweave hand pick-up; the ground-only draft and logo pick-up charts are omitted under the existing catalog policy.
- From Jacquard to Rigid Heddle (pp. 25-28) includes illustrative pickup/threading diagrams but no complete numeric project draft.
- Yarn Lab (pp. 72-75) shows eight fabric samples without numeric threading/tie-up/treadling; no draft is inferred from those photographs.
- The subscriber-exclusive projects pictured on p. 77 require separate project downloads and do not supply numeric drafts in this issue.

Colors approximate printed yarn keys. Length-only repeats, random color ordering, supplementary manipulation, finishing, and pile cutting are stated explicitly rather than replaced with invented numerical data.
